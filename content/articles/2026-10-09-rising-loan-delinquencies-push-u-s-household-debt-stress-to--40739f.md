American households face their most severe debt servicing difficulties in fifteen years, as a surge in overdue obligations pushes delinquency rates toward levels last observed during the aftermath of the 2008 financial crisis. According to the Federal Reserve’s triennial Survey of Consumer Finances, nearly one in five families had fallen behind on loan payments by the end of 2025, underscoring growing vulnerability beneath headline economic growth.

## Market Overview

The Federal Reserve's assessment revealed that the share of households trailing on debt payments jumped roughly 67 percent, rising from 12 percent in 2022 to nearly 20 percent by late 2025. Severe distress also expanded markedly, with families overdue by at least two months escalating from 5 percent to more than 8 percent. This deterioration in repayment performance marks the steepest challenge for consumer solvency since the 2010 survey, when the United States was contending with the fallout of the Great Recession.

Balance-sheet pressure is directly linked to higher debt service burdens relative to earnings. The proportion of households dedicating more than 40 percent of their income to debt payments climbed to 8.6 percent, up from 6.5 percent in 2022, touching its highest level since 2013. These findings align with recent sentiment data from the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, which indicated households view their financial condition as deteriorating both over the prior year and heading into the year ahead.

## Macroeconomic Implications

The sharp erosion in consumer debt performance signals headwinds for aggregate demand, credit portfolios, and monetary policy transmission. While the broader economy sustained expansion alongside the highest inflation rates since the early 1980s, income and wealth trajectories diverged sharply across demographic and generational tiers. Real median household income advanced 7 percent, yet average income dropped 6 percent, reflecting reduced earnings among top earners alongside selective gains at lower deciles. 

Younger working cohorts exhibited distinct strain: families headed by individuals aged 35 to 44 experienced a 25 percent decline in income, pressured heavily by diminished capital gains. Concurrently, racial disparities persisted, with median and mean incomes falling for Black non-Hispanic and Asian households. For the broader economy, elevated debt-to-income thresholds limit the capacity of consumers to absorb borrowing costs, which threatens to restrict consumer credit conditions, temper personal consumption expenditures, and increase repayment stress across banking institutions.

## Key Data & Forecast

- Delinquency rate: The share of families behind on loan payments rose to nearly 20% in late 2025 from approximately 12% in 2022.
- Severe delinquencies: Households late by two months or more increased from 5% to more than 8%, the highest since 2010.
- Debt burden: Families with payment-to-income ratios exceeding 40% expanded from 6.5% to 8.6%, the highest since 2013.
- Income shifts: Real median family income rose 7%, while average family income fell 6%; cohorts aged 35 to 44 logged a 25% decline.
- Net worth totals: Median net worth rose 2% to $215,900, while average net worth reached $1.24 million; net worth for the lowest income quartile dropped 6%.
- Outlook: Elevated repayment obligations and expanding delinquency rates are expected to dampen consumer spending and elevate retail credit risks in upcoming quarters.
