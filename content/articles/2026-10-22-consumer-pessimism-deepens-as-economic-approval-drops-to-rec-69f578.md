A sharp deterioration in household economic sentiment has pushed presidential economic approval ratings to an all-time survey low, with 80 percent of Americans now rating national conditions as fair or poor. According to the latest CNBC All-America Economic Survey of 1,000 registered voters, persistent inflation across groceries and fuel has expanded voter preference for Democratic congressional control to a 52 percent to 45 percent margin just two weeks ahead of the November midterm elections.

## Market Overview

The survey highlights an accelerating erosion in consumer confidence, marked by an expansion of economic pessimism into the president's political base. Overall, 63 percent of respondents express a negative outlook regarding the economy's trajectory, driven by a six-percentage-point increase in Republican pessimism to 35 percent—the highest level recorded across Donald Trump's two presidential terms. 

Trump's net economic approval dropped nine points since July to minus 31 percentage points, with approval falling to 34 percent against 65 percent disapproval. This represents the steepest economic dissatisfaction recorded in the survey's tracking history, surpassing peak disapproval figures registered during the prior administration. Overall presidential approval remained subdued at 40 percent against 59 percent disapproval, generating a net rating of minus 19 percentage points compared to minus 8 points a year ago.

Underpinning this discontent is sustained household budget strain. Fifty-seven percent of voters identify the aggregate costs of food, fuel, and housing as their primary anxiety, with 80 percent reporting rising prices over the past year and 55 percent stating that inflation is accelerating faster than normal. Food costs have emerged as a more acute concern than fuel, cited by 34 percent of respondents compared to 23 percent for gasoline.

## Macroeconomic Implications

The souring sentiment carries substantial ramifications for federal fiscal policy, trade agendas, and legislative power. Voters increasingly favor Democratic management across core cost-of-living concerns, expanding the party's lead on grocery inflation to 16 percentage points from seven points in July, while also shifting voter trust toward Democrats on employment and fuel costs. Concurrently, shifting sentiment in eight critical Senate battleground states—where voters now favor Democratic control by four points after carrying the states for Trump by a combined 10 points in 2024—signals potential legislative gridlock that could constrain future fiscal measures.

Broader economic assessments are also being weighed down by geopolitical frictions. Public endorsement of military engagement in Iran has declined to 46 percent, down from 53 percent in April, with only 35 percent affirming the intervention was justified in light of subsequent energy price spikes. A divided or opposition-controlled Congress would likely subject administrative tariff policies, regulatory changes, and executive spending initiatives to intense oversight and statutory resistance.

## Key Data & Forecast

- Economic Approval: Fell to 34% approval versus 65% disapproval, setting a series record of net -31%.
- Consumer Assessment: 80% rate the economy as fair or poor; 63% express overall economic pessimism.
- Inflation Metrics: 57% cite food, gas, and housing costs as their top priority; 55% view prices as rising faster than normal.
- Legislative Balance: Democrats lead congressional preference 52% to 45% nationally and hold a 4-point lead across eight decisive Senate battlegrounds.
- Outlook: Disproportionate voter engagement among Democrats (72%) compared to Republicans (62%) signals headwinds for the governing party, though base consolidation suggests closely contested margins across swing districts.
