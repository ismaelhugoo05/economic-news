Asian stock benchmarks advanced on Tuesday, led by a sharp rebound in regional semiconductor manufacturers following an initial public offering filing by artificial intelligence firm Anthropic. The filing renewed global risk appetite across the hardware supply chain, providing momentum ahead of upcoming central bank rate decisions and pivotal corporate earnings reports.

## Market Overview

The technology-heavy South Korean KOSPI posted the region's sharpest advance, rising over 3% as semiconductor bellwethers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix jumped 4% and 6%, respectively. Broader domestic sentiment was underpinned by macroeconomic data showing South Korea’s gross domestic product expanded by 1.0% quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter, surpassing consensus projections of 0.7% and accelerating from 0.6% in the second quarter. 

Japanese equities mirrored the positive tone. The Nikkei 225 gained 1.6%, while the broader TOPIX moved 0.6% higher. Key tech-related names saw aggressive buying, with SoftBank Group advancing more than 5% and semiconductor-testing equipment maker Advantest surging nearly 10%. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 added 0.5%.

Conversely, Greater China bourses diverged from the regional upswing. Mainland benchmarks saw the Shanghai Composite and the CSI 300 each drop 0.2%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index retreated 0.6%, though domestic foundries SMIC and Hua Hong Semiconductor recorded slight gains. Singapore’s Straits Times Index slipped 0.2%.

The moves in Asia tracked overnight strength on Wall Street, where the S&P 500 rose 0.7%, the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.8%, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index surged 3%—its most pronounced single-session advance in over a month. 

## Macroeconomic Implications

Anthropic’s public filing brought back into focus the heavy capital expenditure required to scale artificial intelligence infrastructure against current commercial returns. With mega-cap firms such as Alphabet, Microsoft, and Amazon scheduled to release quarterly reports, markets are seeking confirmation that enterprise data center and hardware outlays are translating into durable top-line growth.

This resurgence in capital-intensive tech valuations comes at a critical juncture for global monetary policy. The Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank, and the Bank of Japan are all slated to deliver policy decisions this week. While current expectations point toward unchanged policy rates across all three institutions, forward guidance will be scrutinized to assess the longevity of elevated borrowing costs. Prolonged restrictive rates present ongoing valuation headwinds for high-multiple growth equities. Additionally, while crude oil slipped from recent six-month highs amid diplomatic negotiations in the Middle East, ongoing security risks near Saudi energy facilities maintain an underlying floor for headline energy prices, threatening to prolong global disinflation paths.
