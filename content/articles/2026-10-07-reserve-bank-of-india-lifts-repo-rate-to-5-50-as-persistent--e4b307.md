The Reserve Bank of India increased its benchmark repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% on Wednesday, executing its first monetary tightening move since 2023 to curb accelerating domestic price pressures. The Monetary Policy Committee concurrently altered its stance to "calibrated tightening" following ten straight months of rising consumer costs. Sovereign debt and equity markets adjusted swiftly to the tighter policy setting: yields on benchmark 10-year government bonds climbed 5 basis points to 7.243%, while the Nifty 50 equity index fell 0.7%. Confirming that policy easing is off the table, RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra warned that "inflation and its outlook are not benign, as they were last year" and stressed that upcoming decisions will strictly involve either further hikes or pauses.

## Macro Context

The RBI's policy turnaround underscores the fragile balance between domestic economic expansion and severe imported price shocks. While India recorded a robust 7.8% GDP expansion in the June quarter—outpacing slowing activity across the United States, China, and Japan—inflation dynamics have breached the central bank's comfort zone. Headline retail inflation reached 4.8% in August, exceeding the statutory 4.0% medium-term target, with the monetary authority projecting headline inflation to settle at 5.2% and core prices at 4.4% for the fiscal year ending March 2027. Although the central bank lifted its fiscal-year growth projection by 40 basis points to 7.1%, it acknowledged that protracted geopolitical rifts and elevated international commodity costs threaten the outlook.

India's external vulnerability stems substantially from its structural dependence on overseas energy, importing roughly 85% of its crude oil requirements via corridors exposed to the conflict involving Iran. This supply sensitivity arrives alongside domestic climate risks: the World Bank reported that India registered its fourth-driest June-to-August period since 1960 under prevailing El Niño conditions, heightening the risk of agricultural shortages and food-price spikes. The World Bank anticipates annual economic growth will moderate to 7.1% for the fiscal year ending March 2027 from 7.8% in the prior year.

Globally, New Delhi's decision reflects a broader, coordinated wave of monetary restraint. Central banks across South Korea and Europe have implemented rate increases over the past two months, while the U.S. Federal Reserve enacted its first rate hike in over three years and the Bank of Japan pushed benchmark rates to a 31-year high. By raising borrowing costs, the RBI aims to defend real yields and preserve foreign investment inflows against deteriorating global terms of trade.
